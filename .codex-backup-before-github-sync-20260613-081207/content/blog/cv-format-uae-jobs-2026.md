# CV Format for UAE Jobs (2026): What Employers Actually Expect

The best CV format for UAE jobs is simple, clear, and practical. Recruiters in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, and the wider GCC often receive hundreds of CVs for one role. They do not have time to decode a creative design. They need to see your name, target role, contact number, visa status, experience, skills, and availability quickly.

This guide explains what employers usually expect in 2026. It is written for fresh graduates, skilled workers, hospitality staff, admin applicants, drivers, finance staff, engineers, and other job seekers who want a professional CV without confusion.

<!--cta-->

## The best UAE CV structure

Use this order for most UAE job applications:

1. Name and contact details
2. Target job title
3. Professional summary
4. Personal details if useful: nationality, visa status, location, driving license
5. Work experience
6. Skills
7. Education
8. Certifications and licenses
9. Languages
10. References available upon request

This format works because it gives recruiters the information they normally check first. For UAE employers, visa status and availability can matter. If you are on a visit visa and available immediately, say it clearly. If you are on an employment visa with 30 days notice, write that.

## Should your UAE CV have a photo?

For many UAE roles, a professional photo is common. This is especially true for hospitality, retail, sales, receptionist, customer service, and domestic service roles. It is less important for software, finance, and multinational corporate roles where ATS screening may come first.

If you include a photo, use a clear head-and-shoulders image. Avoid filters, sunglasses, group photos, car selfies, and busy backgrounds. Your photo should look like you are ready for work.

## Personal details employers often check

In Western CV advice, personal details are often removed. In the UAE and GCC, some details are still commonly requested by employers and recruiters. You can include them if they help your application:

| Detail | When to include it |
| --- | --- |
| Nationality | Common in UAE recruitment forms |
| Visa status | Useful if availability matters |
| Current location | Helpful for interview scheduling |
| Driving license | Important for driver, sales, site, and logistics roles |
| Languages | Very important in hospitality, retail, and customer service |

Do not include passport number, Emirates ID number, or private documents in the CV. Share documents only when a trusted employer requests them later.

## Keep the CV one to two pages

Most UAE CVs should be one or two pages. One page is enough for fresh graduates, helpers, cashiers, drivers, junior hospitality workers, and candidates with short experience. Two pages are fine for engineers, nurses, accountants, managers, and experienced technical workers.

Do not make a five-page CV unless the job is senior and your experience truly requires it. Recruiters prefer focused information.

## Use a simple file name

Name your file clearly:

`Mohammed_Ali_Accountant_CV.pdf`

Avoid names like `new cv final final 2.pdf`. A clean file name looks professional and helps recruiters find your CV later.

## ATS-friendly formatting

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems. ATS software reads your CV and checks keywords before a recruiter sees it. Use normal text, standard headings, and simple bullet points. Avoid tables, heavy graphics, text boxes, and unreadable fonts.

Use headings like Professional Summary, Work Experience, Skills, Education, Certifications, Languages, and References. These headings are easy for both humans and software.

## Final checklist

Before you send your CV, check:

- Your phone number includes country code.
- Your email is professional.
- Your job title matches the job you want.
- Your visa status is clear if relevant.
- Your experience has dates and employer names.
- Your skills match the job posting.
- Your PDF opens correctly on mobile.
